# Findings memo: where Chime's complaint handling differs from peers

**To:** Head of Complaints & Customer Operations, Chime Financial Inc

**Data:** CFPB consumer complaints, Jan 2024 – Dec 2025, three products (credit card, checking or savings account, money transfer / virtual currency). Chime: 11,305 complaints. Peers: the other banks, fintechs and similar companies in the dataset.

**Basis:** the "outlier-adjusted" view, which leaves out months with an unusual complaint volume. All-months figures are given where they differ.

## Summary

Chime answers complaints on time (100% timely response), but a much smaller share of its complaints end in relief for the customer. That gap shows up in every product, every large state and most issue types, and it is largest in "Money Transfer, Virtual Currency or Money Service". The data shows where the gap is, not why it exists.

## Where customers complain

"Checking and savings accounts" account for 72% of Chime’s complaints, "Money Transfer, Virtual Currency or Money Service" for 19% and "Credit Card" for 9%. Within "Checking or Savings Account", “Managing an account” is the largest issue at 53% of complaints, followed by “Problem with a lender or other company charging your account” (23%) and “Closing an account” (13%). The findings below focus on how those complaints end, since that is where Chime differs most from its peers.

## Top 3 findings

**1. Chime's relief rate is about half the peer rate: 10.1% vs 23.2%.**
Across all months it is 10.2% vs 18.8%. Chime ranks 33rd of 51 banks and fintechs with at least 500 complaints, below the median company (about 21%) but not the lowest. The gap holds in every product:

| Product | Chime | Peers |
|---|---|---|
| Credit card | 13.5% | 30.2% |
| Checking or savings account | 10.9% | 20.3% |
| Money transfer / virtual currency | 5.2% | 11.2% |

**2. The gap is not a regional effect.**
In all 30 states where Chime has at least 100 complaints, its relief rate is below the peer rate. In the outlier-adjusted view the gap is 7.6 to 19.4 percentage points. Across all months it is smaller in some states (down to 0.6 points) but still negative everywhere.

**3. Money transfer is the weakest area, and one issue stands out.**
"Other transaction problem" is the largest money transfer issue for Chime (about a third of its money transfer complaints) and ends in relief in only 1.5% of cases (5.3% when October 2025 is excluded). "Fraud or scam" and "unauthorized transactions" together make up most of the rest, with relief at 6% and 8%, below peers.

## One event to explain

In October 2025 Chime received 1,463 complaints in a single month, with 735 in money transfer and 553 in "Other transaction problem", peaking on 6–7 October. Relief that month was 4.4%. The data does not show the cause. The outlier-adjusted view flags unusual months across the whole market, so this Chime-specific spike is **not** removed from the figures above. It lowers Chime's money transfer relief rate but does not explain the gap on its own ("Credit Card" and "Checking or Savings Account" are also below peers) and it should be explained internally.

## Recommendations

1. **Review "Other transaction problem" cases in money transfer** to see why so few end in relief, and whether they are being closed without resolution or are mostly cases where no remedy applies.
2. **Compare outcomes on fraud, scam and unauthorized transaction complaints with a stronger peer** to see whether relief policy, rather than case mix, explains the gap.
3. **Run a post-mortem on October 2025** and set an alert on weekly complaint volume so a spike is spotted within days.
4. **Set a target for relief rate by product** and review it monthly.

## Limits

- Complaints are self-reported and are not a random sample of customers.
- Volume of complaints may depend on company size and events.
- A low relief rate can reflect the mix of complaints (more cases where no remedy is owed) as well as how they are handled. The data cannot separate the two.
- There is no response date in the data, so response speed in days cannot be measured, only the timely flag.
- US data only.
